How it works

A contrail lasts when the air is colder than about −40 °C at cruise height (the Schmidt-Appleman criterion) and supersaturated with respect to ice, so its crystals do not evaporate. These humid layers sit mostly at 8 to 12 km and are often only a few hundred metres deep, so a change of about 2,000 ft (600 m) avoids most of them. Lasting contrails warm the climate on average; flying away from the best cruise level, FL340 here, burns more fuel.

Drag up or down on the sky, or use the buttons. Keyboard: up and down arrows (or W and S) change level, Space starts, P pauses. Score: 1000 minus contrail share of the route and extra fuel.
