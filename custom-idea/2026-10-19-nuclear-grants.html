Custom Grant Path: nuclear war research

A quick targeted scan for funding that fits Ananth’s nuclear war climate research: nuclear winter, soot and smoke lofted into the stratosphere, and the impacts on climate, crops and food security. The best nuclear-war fits come first, and policy-only funds are labelled as such. 21 entries. Eligibility tags assume Ananth as Applicant PI or project lead (PhD December 2022, fixed-term postdoc at Exeter). GO only when the call text allows it, MAYBE when the text or his contract still needs confirming. Companion to the 19 October Grant Path.

Nuclear war climate research funders

Nuclear Weapons Policy Fund

  • Funder: Longview Philanthropy
  • Deadline: No dated round — no open application form on the fund page; contact the nuclear grantmaking team (info@longview.org)
  • Eligibility: MAYBE — the fund page does not say who may apply or how, so eligibility for a UK university postdoc is not stated; it would be a direct approach.
  • Why it might fit: Strong on topic. Besides deterrence and arms control, the page lists “consequences of nuclear use” and “civilizational resilience” among the other areas it considers. It is the only nuclear funder found today that names consequences.
  • Why it might not fit: Policy-led: its three mechanisms are applied policy research, Track 1.5/2 dialogues and talent for government roles. A pure nuclear-winter modelling project would need a clear decision-maker use.

https://www.longview.org/fund/nuclear-weapons-policy-fund/

NATO Science for Peace and Security: call 2027-1

  • Funder: NATO SPS Programme
  • Deadline: Full proposals by Sunday 15 November 2026, 23:59 CET (22:59 UK); funding decisions can take up to nine months
  • Eligibility: MAYBE — no career-stage rule, and academic co-directors are allowed, but every bid needs a NATO-country Project Director (he could be the UK one) and a Partner-country Project Director employed in an eligible partner country, such as Australia, New Zealand, Japan, Switzerland, Ireland or Austria. Co-directors are limited to one proposal per call.
  • Why it might fit: Partial. A Multi-Year Project (typically about €400k over 36 months) or an Event (Advanced Research Workshop) on the security consequences of a nuclear war, for example food and energy resilience under sunlight loss. This call’s topics include protecting critical infrastructure and supplies, and keeping facilities running through prolonged disruption.
  • Why it might not fit: The call needs a clear security link and expects projects to reach Technology Readiness Levels 3–6 with end-user letters, which fits applied tools better than climate science. An Event is the more realistic route, and the partner co-director must be found in four weeks.

https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/sps/grants/SPS-Call4proposals-2027-1.pdf

Consortium to Reduce Nuclear Dangers (second call)

  • Funder: Carnegie Corporation of New York (host), with Longview, Founders Pledge, Global Challenges Foundation, PAX sapiens and Global Risks Fund
  • Deadline: Closed 29 May 2026 (decisions late autumn/winter 2026); a third call has not been announced
  • Eligibility: GO — the call text accepts individual researchers and university centres, inside and outside the US, and encourages early- and mid-career applicants, especially on the small-grant tier. A nonprofit (501(c)(3) or equivalent) base is “strongly preferred”, and indirect costs are capped at 15%.
  • Why it might fit: Partial. Small grants of $50k–$200k for up to two years. Focus area 2, “Verification, Monitoring, and Risk Transparency”, names “modeling and simulation tools” and forecasting, which is the nearest door for consequences modelling. Watch for a third call.
  • Why it might not fit: Policy-only in practice: projects need a theory of change that reduces nuclear risk, and “academic publishing … without a defined uptake strategy” is not prioritised. There is no open window now.

https://carnegie.org/article/second-call-for-proposals-grants-to-support-reducing-nuclear-dangers/

Ploughshares Fund grants

  • Funder: Ploughshares Fund
  • Deadline: Fall 2026 cycle closed; next cycle dates “will likely be posted in January 2027”. Discretionary grants of up to $50,000 between board meetings, by inquiry
  • Eligibility: MAYBE — Ploughshares makes grants to individuals and international organisations, but it “does not cover overhead costs at academic institutions”, so Exeter would have to accept a no-overhead award.
  • Why it might fit: Partial. Nuclear threat reduction is its whole remit; a short evidence piece on the humanitarian and climate consequences of nuclear use for a policy audience could fit a discretionary grant.
  • Why it might not fit: An advocacy and policy funder, not a science funder; it does not fund dissertations or books, and the no-overhead rule may block a university award.

https://ploughshares.org/grants/apply/

Nuclear Deterrence Fund (Knowledge Accelerator Pathway)

  • Funder: Ministry of Defence
  • Deadline: No dated open round on GOV.UK; applications and research areas via Contracts Finder; all current projects must finish by March 2027
  • Eligibility: MAYBE — the Knowledge Accelerator Pathway is written for doctoral and postdoctoral researchers wanting to be PI on a short project (typically under a year), so his career stage fits. Today’s Grant Path tagged it NO-GO on remit; it is MAYBE here only on eligibility.
  • Why it might fit: Weak to partial. The fund wants nuclear-deterrence policy evidence. A project on how climate and food consequences bear on UK deterrence policy, or how they are perceived, is the only bridge.
  • Why it might not fit: Policy-only: every funded project listed is strategy, perceptions or escalation, with no physical-science or climate-effects work. With projects ending by March 2027, a new award would run under five months.

https://www.gov.uk/guidance/nuclear-deterrence-fund

Survival and Flourishing Fund (rolling application and Speculation Grants)

  • Funder: Survival and Flourishing Fund
  • Deadline: SFF-2026 main and theme rounds closed; the rolling application stays open, and late applications are still eligible for Speculation Grants and the next round (not yet announced)
  • Eligibility: MAYBE — SFF recommends funding to organisations and charities; an individual needs a fiscal sponsor (Exeter could act as the host). Whether a university project is welcome was not confirmed today.
  • Why it might fit: Partial. A catastrophic-risk funder with $20–40M a year across rounds; nuclear-war consequences and resilience research has the right shape for it.
  • Why it might not fit: Nuclear war is not named on the 2026 round page, the next round has no date, and the S-Process takes six to eight months.

https://survivalandflourishing.fund/2026/application

Global Catastrophic Risks Opportunities fund [unverified]

  • Deadline: Report due to the UN General Assembly in 2027 (82nd session)
