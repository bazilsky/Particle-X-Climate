How it works

Cloud droplets stay liquid well below 0 °C. A pure droplet freezes on its own near −38 °C (homogeneous freezing). An ice-nucleating particle lets ice form at warmer temperatures: some bacteria and pollen from about −2 °C, mineral dust such as K-feldspar from about −15 to −25 °C, while soot is a poor seed that works only from about −30 °C.

Press Freeze near the middle of the particle's range. Too warm and no ice forms; too late and the droplet freezes on its own. The first two droplets show the range on the thermometer. Keyboard: Space freezes or starts, P pauses. You can also tap the cloud.
